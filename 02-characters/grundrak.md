# Grundrak — the dwarf empiricist

**Account genre:** political thriller
**Register:** driving, vindicated, with a buried hollowness that surfaces only at his apparent triumph.

## Role in the system
The applied empiricist who first reads dragon dung as *an ore nobody thought to
assay*. Dwarves are already the archetype of extraction, processing and combustion,
so his interest is **professional, not intellectual** — he doesn't find it
disgusting or demeaning; he finds it unexamined. He becomes the engine of
industrialisation and, eventually, the holder of real power behind a ceremonial
crown.

## Arc
Curiosity → trial-by-competition against the mages → vindication → revolution
(the throne-room coup) → rule. His circle *completes* — he gets everything he set
out to get — and that completion is the trap.

## What he sees
- The system's logic, clearly and before anyone else.
- Class: he reads the mages' resistance as guild self-protection, and his own rise
  as overdue justice. A Marxist framing without the vocabulary.

## What he's structurally blind to
- **The hollowness of his win.** On the throne, having proved everything, the
  satisfaction is enormous and already fading. He doesn't want the throne; he wants
  the throne *to not matter* — but by taking it he proves it still does. He's used
  the master's house to dismantle the master's house. He cannot see this; the King
  can (see [[the-king]]), and notes that Grundrak *sat in the chair* — the one
  tell that the hierarchy still owns him.

## Key beats (where his arc peaks)
- The trials — his vindication, the chapter's climax.
- The coup — narrated *elsewhere* (the King's chapter). In Grundrak's own account
  the coup may be anticlimax: the thing he wanted, curdling as he gets it.

## Why he's humanised early and unseen later
He narrates the trials himself, so the reader meets him as multifaceted, capable of
empathy, but resentful. That earned humanity is exactly why he works as an *unseen
antagonist* in the King's chapter — we already know his motives, so his absence
reads as menace, not mystery.

## Voice notes
- Concrete, materials-first metaphors (ore, assay, load, charge).
- Contempt expressed as precision, not heat.

## Open questions
- How much of the hollowness does *he* articulate vs. how much only the reader sees?
- His child, in the strategic marriage — does Grundrak see the marriage as victory or
  as his own absorption into the dynastic order he despised?
