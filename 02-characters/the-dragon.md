# The Dragon — the source

**Account genre:** undecided — "something that hasn't quite decided what genre it is
yet," which is the point. Fable-from-outside shading toward horror.
**Register:** sardonic, menacing, outside all human categories; formally **rhymed**
with Dalla's account (see [[dalla]]).

## Role in the system
**The source.** Her metabolic output is the entire economy; therefore the economy
**owns her body**. She is the productive centre and, for exactly that reason,
captive — contained not by chains but by **comfort and incentive**. The outermost
narrative frame: the view from outside the human drama.

## Arc
Wild predator → husbanded, fed, plumbed, scheduled → thirty years of comfort →
metabolic decline → appetite redirected → gaze turning predatory again. Her circle
threatens to *reopen* — the only character whose ending is genuinely unresolved.

## What she sees
- The infrastructure changing around her (reinforced plumbing, altered schedules)
  the way a farm animal notices its pen — **without** grasping that her own weight
  gain is the cause.
- People who think they own her, watching her. (This is the observational rhyme with
  Dalla.)

## What she's structurally blind to
- That she is the diabetic externality of her own success — though by the end the
  *body* knows it even if the mind narrates it as restlessness.
- Dalla. The system has done the same thing to both of them; neither knows the other.

## The diabetes hinge
Comfort is making her ill. The dissatisfaction is **embodied, not philosophical** —
the feeding regime sickened her, and sickness redirects appetite back toward humans
("long pig not pig" — she's been comparing flavour profiles taxonomically, so this
is an *emerging preference*, not an impulse). The reduced collection schedule turns
this logistical: the prey is becoming scarce through the system's normal operation,
giving the threat a **timeline**.

## The unresolved ending
Returning to hunting humans can be framed as her planned **"get-fit routine."** Leave
it open whether she'll seek the challenge of the old ways or stay in her comfort
zone. The return-to-the-familiar (dragons eating humans) is the same as the starting
condition **but worse**, because the humans are now proximate, complacent and
dependent on the very thing that will destroy them.

## Fossil-fuel allegory (keep buried)
A civilisation built on a biological energy source that seemed an unlimited gift,
whose success creates the conditions for catastrophe. Her obesity is the unpriced
externality. **Never state it.** The mechanism carries it; the moment it's spelled
out it becomes the Boxer problem.

## Voice notes
- Cold amusement; the contempt of something that pre-dates the entire economy built
  on it.
- Bodily, sensory, taxonomic about food.
- Share sentence-rhythms and "being watched" asides with Dalla — close, not identical.

## Open questions
- First person throughout, or does she only get the retrospective outer frame?
- How much veterinary/keeper relationship to dramatise — conflicted loyalty of whoever
  tends her?
- Does she get a name? (Likely not — pre-linguistic to the human order.)
