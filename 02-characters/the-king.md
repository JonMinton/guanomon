# The King — the deposed sovereign

**Account genre:** horror (the horror of waking inside someone else's revolution)
**Register:** cold, analytical, precise; a man who has replayed the same night a
thousand times.

## Role in the system
The holder of *formal* sovereignty who is reduced to a **ceremonial figurehead** —
nominally in charge while the dwarves hold real power. His chapter carries the
**coup** (replacing the retired Arrath Keep siege) and, a decade on, the **colonial
expansion**.

## Arc
Sovereignty → the demonstration brought to his door → strategic submission →
ceremonial survival → complicity in expansion. He narrates his own obsolescence in
real time, and every tool he has for resisting it — dynastic marriage, bloodline
politics, courtly protocol — is itself absorbed into the new order. That's the
horror: nothing he reaches for is outside the thing consuming him.

## What he sees
- **Everything, and that's the curse.** He sees Grundrak at his side rather than
  behind him; mages who once outranked dwarves now presenting intelligence like
  junior staff; his own child's naturalised deference to the new hierarchy while he
  himself still holds discriminatory attitudes toward dwarves.
- The one tell: Grundrak **sat in the chair.** A man who truly didn't care about the
  hierarchy wouldn't have needed to. The King files this for later — his only
  remaining form of power is *noticing*.

## The coup (narrated only here)
Woken by detonations at night; the palace walls — like Arrath Keep's forty feet of
granite — broken by guano. Grundrak on the throne before the King has finished
dressing. The King *chooses* to dress: vanity, yes, and also the **last act of
sovereignty available to him**, performed knowingly and futilely. Grundrak's line:
*"You didn't come to the demonstration, so I decided to bring the demonstration to
you."*

> Tell it **only** from the King's perspective. Grundrak is already humanised in his
> own chapter; here he should be an unseen, inevitable antagonist — the path of
> devastation from outer wall to throne *is the argument*: there is nothing between
> us that I cannot go through.

## The taxonomy of submission (his place in it)
Three characters, three mechanisms, one system:
- **The King — hard power.** The demonstration *is* the threat; he does the
  arithmetic on his own walls.
- **Dalla — soft power / incentive.** Status that entombs.
- **The Dragon — soft power / incentive.** Comfort that contains.

The King's submission to *hard* power rhymes with Dalla's and the Dragon's
submission to *soft* power.

## A decade on
Throne room, King a decade older, Grundrak beside him, mages as analysts reporting
on neighbours' resources (dragons, fertile land, gems) to choose the next
"demonstration." A strategic marriage between Grundrak's child and the King's — from
the King's view a forced dynastic concession; the children themselves are already
used to each other, the King's child already supplicant to dwarves.

## Voice notes
- Forensic exactness about times, counts, routes — the precision of obsessive replay.
- Analytical distance as the last dignity.

## Open questions
- Does the King get a name, or does anonymity reinforce "the institution"?
- How much does he *understand* the unit/source rhyme that binds him to Dalla and the
  Dragon — or is that strictly the reader's to assemble?
