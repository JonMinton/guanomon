# Marga — the shoveller (the Guano Guild's human face)

**Account genre:** working-class realism / naturalism — pointedly **the un-genre.** Where
every other account performs a genre (thriller, elegy, horror, corporate report), Marga's
refuses to. It's just the work, seen plainly — and that refusal *is* her characterisation:
she's selling nothing, so hers is the account the reader trusts most, the ground truth the
others' self-dramatisations are measured against.
**Register:** dry, physical, unsentimental, gallows-humoured. The body's-eye view — weight,
smell, ache, the knot squelching under the fingers. Funny the way people are funny when the
work is awful and constant.

## Role in the system — the face the Guild doesn't have
One of the **collection crew** from Scene 1 (with Aldric and Kael), now a full account. She
is the **proletarian POV the story otherwise lacks** — the people who actually shovel the
muck, on whom the whole economy rests and who are everywhere faceless. She is also the
**human body of the [[the-guild|Guano Guild]] itself**: the title institution speaks as a
faceless [[the-guild|corporate voice]], but its *truth* is Marga's hands in the dung. So the
Guild now has **two voices in opposite registers** — the laundered annual report (maximal
lie) and the shoveller's realism (maximal truth). Same institution; the Rashomon engine
running through one guild. (Fills the collection-crew gap in [[guilds]].)

## Pays off Scene 1
The cold-open crew — **Marga** (shovelling), **Aldric** (the watcher, the veteran), **Kael**
(the wry one, *"another day in paradise"*) — return here named and known. The prologue's
anonymous labour gets a face and an interior; on a reread, Scene 1 is revealed as *her shift.*
Answers the standing structural question (does the crew reappear?) — **yes, as Marga's
account** ([[plot-beats]]).

## The Dalla rhyme (load-bearing, quiet)
Marga and [[dalla|Dalla]] began in the same place: a raiding crew, going down into the dung.
**Dalla fell once** — the freak accident — and became a unit of measurement and a broadcast
star. **Marga didn't fall** — she just kept shovelling, anonymous, for twenty years. The
lottery of one accident sorted two near-identical women into "the [[dalla|Dalla]]" and a
nobody. Marga knows of Dalla (everyone does, via the [[the-hum|Hum]]) and has a flat,
unimpressed view: *she fell in it once; I've been in it every day since, and nobody named a
unit after me.* This reinforces [[dalla|Dalla's]] lampshade **from below** — the worker's-eye
correction to the founder-myth.

## What she sees (the clearest eyes in the book)
- The **actual cost**: the bodies, the [[labour-and-order|new lethal work]] (drying-station
  blasts, the apiary dead, the [[dern|Peacekeepers']] truncheons) seen from the tier that
  absorbs it. Her account is where the abstractions of [[labour-and-order]] become specific
  funerals.
- The system **from the bottom, without myth.** No founder-story to protect, no craft to
  mourn, no throne to lose — so she sees the machine plainly. The one narrator with no
  illusions.

## What she's blind to (or won't look at)
- Her own **structural importance** — that she and her kind hold up the whole edifice, and
  that the labour could be leverage. Whether she *can't* see it or *won't* (fatalism vs.
  unawareness) is a live choice; **fatalism is richer** and keeps determinism intact (she
  knows and shrugs, because what would it change?).
- The grand frames everyone else drowns in (the coup, the Hum's meaning, the dragons) reach
  her as rumour and weather, not drama. The big narratives don't include her; she returns the
  indifference.

## Optional: a micro old/new tension inside the crew
If useful, run the old/new pattern at the labour tier too: **Aldric** is old enough to have
been a *real treasure-adventurer* (the [[guilds|Adventurers' Guild]] before its target
"changed from treasure to guano") and remembers raiding dragons for *gold*; Marga (or Kael)
is younger, born to shovelling, and never knew it. The fantasy-deflation in one crew — *they
once raided dragons for treasure; now they shovel dragon dung for the Guild.* **Don't force
it:** the chapter may be stronger as pure ground-truth than as a fourth iteration of the
old/new motif. Flag and decide later.

## Voice notes
- Concrete and bodily; smell and weight do the work. Present-tense-feeling even in retrospect.
- Gallows humour, never self-pity. Kael supplies the jokes, Aldric the quiet, Marga the eye.
- **Not** ennobled or pitied — that would be its own condescension. She's competent, funny,
  tired, and right.

## Open questions
- Confirm **Marga** as the POV (vs Aldric the veteran or Kael the joker). Marga = the
  hands-on shoveller, the most viscerally "the work" — recommended.
- How much does her account *correct* the others vs simply *ignore* them? (Recommend: she
  rarely engages the grand narratives head-on — the gap between her plainness and their drama
  does the work.)
- One brushed encounter with a named principal (a [[dern|Peacekeeper]] action, [[dalla|Dalla]]
  in person, a [[grundrak|Guild]] inspection) could be devastating; more would over-connect.
- Placement: she pairs powerfully **right after [[the-guild|the Guild's]] laundered account**
  — the corporate lie immediately answered by the worker's truth, same institution. See
  [[plot-beats]].
