# Brask — the alchemy-native mage

> **Not a POV narrator — observed from outside.** Brask is the **least introspective
> character in the book**, so he gets *no* first-person account; his story is told through
> someone else's eyes — **[[vesser|Vesser's second chapter]]** (decided 2026-05-30). What
> follows is a character study to write *him* from, not a voice he narrates in.

**Subject-matter / mode (as seen in Vesser's chapter):** the technical sublime — Brask
treats the [[the-hum|Hum]] the way a coder treats a beautiful build, affect reserved
entirely for the *elegance*, none for the consequence. The chapter's tension is that this
flat brilliance reaches us through Vesser's *elegiac* register: the mourner narrating the
machine that buried him.

> **Name — LOCKED: Brask** *(confirmed 2026-05-30).* Blunt, consonant-heavy, deliberately
> *un*-courtly — unlike [[vesser|Vesser]]'s soft old-world name.

## Role in the system
The younger mage who **invents the Hum** — the mass-communication novum the displaced
mages back into while failing to reproduce guano. Crucially he is **alchemy-native**: he
came up *after* the trials were lost, so he never knew the old regime in which magecraft
was prestige. To him, R&D is not a humiliating demotion (as it is for [[vesser|Vesser]])
— it is simply *the job*, and an exciting one. He has no nostalgia to wound.

He is a **single-minded systems genius — a John Carmack type**: brilliant, obsessive,
literal, and conspicuously lacking the *airs and graces* the old courtly order required.
**The point is that the old regime would have had no place for him** — his temperament
was a disqualification then and is a superpower now. The new world rewards exactly the
person the old world filtered out.

## The structural parallel (load-bearing)
Brask is to the mages what [[the-princess|the Princess]] is to the crown: the **new-world
generation** for whom the old world's grief is unintelligible. The book now carries **two
matched old/new disjunctions**:

- [[the-king|King]] (mourns sovereignty) ↔ [[the-princess|Princess]] (at home in the new order)
- [[vesser|Vesser]] (mourns magecraft) ↔ **Brask** (never knew it; just builds)

Read his chapter *against* Vesser's the way the Princess reads against the King.

## "Brask is brash" — the defining trait (implicit or explicit)
The phrase can sit on the page or just under it. The crucial nuance: **his brashness is
not peacocking.** It is *not* the brashness of a man asserting dominance or performing
status — it is the brashness of someone who **does not notice or care how blunt and direct
he is.** He'll flatly denigrate the expertise and intelligence of other mages and
alchemists — not to wound, not to win, but because their incompetence is simply a *fact* to
him and he sees no reason not to state facts. The absence of malice is what makes it land:
contempt would at least acknowledge you; Brask's bluntness doesn't even register you as
someone who could be offended.

**The tell that proves it isn't tribal: he is the *least* dispositionally dismissive of
dwarves** — provided they're good engineers. He judges on **one axis only: is the work
good?** A competent dwarf earns his straightforward respect; an incompetent mage of his own
kind earns his straightforward dismissal. He is **post-tribal not out of virtue but out of
obliviousness** — he lacks the social radar everyone else is steered by (birth, species,
guild, deference). This quietly makes him the purest embodiment of the new order's value
system (competence over category) — and, accidentally, one of the few humans the dwarves
can genuinely work with. Contrast the [[the-king|King]], who still harbours anti-dwarf
prejudice, and even [[grundrak|Grundrak]], who is acutely class-conscious; Brask simply
can't see the categories.

## What he sees
- The Hum as an exquisite problem solved — resonance, fidelity, reach. He sees the
  **system** with total clarity and genuine joy.
- That signal is cheap and power expensive — he grasps intuitively what Vesser never
  does: the mages were chasing the wrong destiny. He isn't *bitter* about the trials; he
  finds them irrelevant, almost quaint.

## What he's structurally blind to
- **What the Hum is *for*.** He builds the most consequential instrument of the age and
  has no model of its use — that it will manufacture [[dalla|Dalla's]] coerced celebrity,
  broadcast the [[the-guild|Guild's]] laundered myth, and (fused with guano) arm the
  [[story-bible|Hummingbirds]]. The
  Oppenheimer/Carmack blindness: pure builder, indifferent to deployment. As seen in
  [[vesser|Vesser's chapter]] he should read as *cheerful* in a way that curdles for the
  reader, who has the other accounts and knows what his elegant systems are doing to people.
- The cost to Vesser — whom he may regard, without malice, as simply obsolete. He is not
  cruel; he just doesn't see the grief as real, which is its own cruelty.

## Voice notes (his *dialogue*, observed — he doesn't narrate)
- Technical delight; his speech lights up around mechanism and goes flat around people.
- Blunt to the point of rudeness without any awareness of it (see "Brask is brash").
- Never editorialises about power or politics — the *absence* of that register is the
  characterisation. The reader (via Vesser) supplies the dread he doesn't feel.
- Keep him likeable. A monstrous-builder caricature would let the reader off the hook;
  his decency-plus-blindness is the unsettling part.
