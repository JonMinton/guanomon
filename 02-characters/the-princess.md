## The Princess — Old Money adapted to new Circumstances

**Account genre:** *(undecided — candidate: bildungsroman / conversion narrative; the
one account whose arc bends* toward *the new order rather than mourning the old)*

The princess is the daughter of [[the-king]]. She marries one of [[grundrak|the
Dwarf]]'s sons (the strategic marriage that the King narrates as a forced dynastic
concession — see [[the-king]] "A decade on"). She undergoes a faster and more
totalising cultural conversion than her father, as more of her formative years were
spent while dwarven power was in the ascendency.

Her story is an important bridge showing the transition in norms and values as the
power base moves.

## Why she matters structurally
- She is the King's *"child's naturalised deference"* made into a **voice** rather than
  a thing the King merely observes. Where the King experiences the new order as
  [[the-king|horror]], she experiences it as *home* — the same facts, opposite valence.
  That's the Rashomon mechanism, and it may be the cleanest generational version of it.
- She answers [[grundrak|Grundrak's]] open question from the other side: he wonders
  whether the marriage is "victory or his own absorption into the dynastic order he
  despised." Her account is where the reader sees what that absorption *produces* — a
  child of the old order fluent in the new.
- Possible rhyme with [[dalla]] and [[the-dragon]]: a third woman whose body
  (dynastic, reproductive) is an instrument of the system — but, unlike them, she is
  not caged by it; she *adapts* and rises. Worth deciding whether she breaks the
  unit/source rhyme or extends it.

## Open questions
- **Is she a seventh voiced account, or a section within the King's?** The current
  structure ([[plot-beats]]) plans **six** accounts and gestures at a "seventh, silent
  perspective the structure implies but never voices." Making the Princess a full
  account is a real structural change — resolve before locking order.
- What genre is her chapter? (If the King's is horror, hers as *comfortable* inheritance
  is the strongest contrast — possibly the only account without an elegiac undertow.)
- Does she know what was done to [[dalla]] / the Dragon, and does she care? Her
  blindness (or studied incuriosity) could be the engine of her chapter.
