# Dern — the slayer made butcher *(name provisional)*

**Account genre:** war confession / disillusioned soldier's memoir. Bitterer and more
guilt-shadowed than [[vesser|Vesser's]] craft-elegy — closer to a veteran's reckoning
than a mourning.
**Register:** plain, hard, declarative; a man who distrusts fine words because he has
seen what they dress up. Gallows clarity rather than lyricism.

> **Name:** working name *Dern* (short, soldierly). Alternatives to react to: *Garran*,
> *Toval*, *Hessric*, *Kade*. Gender flexible — defaulted male to avoid crowding the
> female-body theme ([[dalla]], [[the-dragon]], [[the-princess]]); a female slayer is a
> live option. Decide and record.

## The opening line (protect this)
The chapter **opens on a confession**: *he once killed a dragon* — and from where he now
stands that seems **insane**, like boasting you once burned down a granary in a famine,
or dynamited a power station for sport. The act that made him a hero in the old world is,
in the new one, **obscene**: you do not kill the thing the whole economy runs on. He
states it flatly, up front, and the reader feels the whole value-inversion of the world
land in one sentence. *(This is the [[genre-theory|novum changing values, not just
conditions]], delivered through a single man's shame.)*

## Role in the system
Representative of the **Slayers' / Warriors' Guild** — the one guild the novum left with
*no premise*. Dragon-slaying was their entire heroic identity and is now vandalism. The
guild is redirected to **projecting force abroad**: subjugating less-advanced neighbours
who haven't industrialised, with guano firepower (the "demonstrations" the
[[the-king|King's]] colonial-expansion beat refers to). See [[story-bible]] (guilds /
Hummingbirds).

## Arc (a double displacement)
Heroism → obsolescence (the dragon role dies) → consolation role (remote force-projection
abroad) → the dawning fear that **even this last role is ending** (Hummingbirds). He is
displaced *twice*: once by the inversion that made slaying shameful, and again, pending,
by the automation that will make *him* unnecessary to killing.

## What he sees
- That his power is now so total it has stopped being **courage**. Crushing
  pre-industrial peoples with guano ordnance isn't battle; it's **remote butchery**. The
  asymmetry that should feel like victory feels like slaughter, and he knows it.
- That heroism required a *fair* enemy, and the new world has abolished fair enemies. His
  grief is for *risk* — the thing that made killing mean something — not for killing.
- By the chapter's end: **this is his only remaining role**, and the
  [[story-bible|Hummingbirds]] may
  take even it. War without soldiers. He will be obsolete in the one thing left to him.

## What he's structurally blind to
- **That the heroism he mourns was always the lie the novum merely exposed.** Slaying
  dragons was *always* ecological vandalism dressed as valour — the world just hadn't
  priced it yet. Like [[vesser|Vesser]], his elegy is self-flattering: he grieves a
  golden age of "honest" violence that was never honest. (Hold the parallel to Vesser
  tight — two men mourning vocations that deserved to die, each unable to see it.)
- The [[the-dragon|Dragon]]: he killed one of *her kind* once and cannot connect that to
  the caged source the economy now milks. The reader holds both; he doesn't.

## Thematic placement — the third displaced incumbent
He completes a **triad of old-world professionals** the novum renders obsolete, each
narrating his own redundancy:
- [[the-king|The King]] — sovereignty
- [[vesser|Vesser]] — magecraft
- **Dern** — heroism / arms
Set against the ascendant new generation ([[grundrak|Grundrak]], [[the-princess|Princess]],
[[brask|Brask]]). Dern is the *martial* voice of that loss, and the only one who has
personally **killed**, which gives his obsolescence a moral weight the others lack.

## Voice notes
- No glory-talk; he has contempt for it. The horror is in his flatness.
- Let the guilt sit under the prose, rarely named. He confesses the dragon once, up
  front, then never pleads.

## Open questions
- Name and gender (see above).
- Does he ever operate / witness a Hummingbird demonstration on-page — the machine that
  will replace him — and is that the chapter's climax?
- How much does he understand the [[the-dragon|Dragon]] connection? Probably none; the
  irony is the reader's. But a single unbidden memory of the dragon he killed, surfacing
  near the end, could be devastating.
