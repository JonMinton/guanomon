# The Guild — the institutional voice

**Account genre:** corporate annual report / official history
**Register:** bureaucratic, smoothing, relentlessly positive; the prose of an
institution describing itself.

## Role in the system
Not a person but a **voice** — the Guild's official account of itself. Its narrative
job is the **normalisation montage**: to take every conflict in every other chapter
and resolve it into a tidy progress narrative.

## Why it's the keystone
It is the **only** narrator that successfully reconciles all the other accounts —
and it does so by **erasing** them. Grundrak's hollow triumph, Vesser's grief,
Dalla's entombment, the King's horror, the Dragon's decline: in the Guild's telling
these become milestones, efficiencies, founder myths, managed transitions. Its
tidiness is precisely the indictment. The reader, arriving at it after the human
accounts, reads every smooth sentence against the cost it conceals.

## What it sees / shows
- Throughput, adoption, infrastructure, growth. The Dalla defined as a clean unit of
  measure. The mages "transitioning" into alchemy. The dragon "well cared for."
- Founder-myth versions of the discovery (Dalla as discoverer, not faller) and of the
  coup (a "reorganisation of governance," perhaps).

## What it cannot see (by design)
- Anyone's interior. Every human cost above is invisible to it, or rendered as a
  positive externality. The blindness is total and is the source of the irony.

## Placement question
The Guild account can sit:
- **Late**, as the penultimate flattening before the Dragon's account reopens
  everything — strongest ironic contrast; the reader has the costs in hand and watches
  them be laundered; or
- **Interleaved**, as recurring institutional interludes between human chapters —
  more relentless, more *Pantheon*-like in its drip of official register.

Decide and record in [[plot-beats]].

## Voice notes
- Nominalisations, passive voice, the agentless sentence ("efficiencies were
  realised").
- Euphemism as a system: "demonstration," "transition," "managed decline."
- Never wink at the reader. The Guild believes itself entirely.

## Open questions
- Is it a single document (a charter, an anniversary report) or an accreting set of
  notices across the decades?
- Does it ever *slip* — one sentence where the cost shows through the euphemism — or
  is its surface perfect?
