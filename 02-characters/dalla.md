# Dalla — the accidental discoverer

**Account genre:** satire of commemoration
**Register:** bitter, watchful, alert to being looked at; should formally *rhyme*
with the Dragon's account (see below).

## Role in the system
She did not discover anything. She **fell**. The discovery was an accident she was
adjacent to, and the system rewarded her with a form of status that **entombs**
rather than liberates: she becomes the **"Dalla,"** the eponymous unit of energy
output. A person converted into a measurement.

## Arc
The fall → the leering → celebrity → the celebrity that *encloses* her. Her circle
closes around her like a cage. There is no clean exit.

## What she sees
- Her own erasure-by-celebration. She is everywhere (as a unit, a name, a face) and
  nowhere (as a person).
- The economics of her own silence: she *could* tell the truth — "I didn't discover
  anything, I fell" — but the truth collapses her only source of status without
  replacing it. The system has made honesty **economically irrational** for her.

## The lampshade is hers (the discovery-gate meta-line)

**Dalla speaks the "why was this never discovered before?" line** — the device that
turns the [[discovery-gating|brief's central flaw]] into a feature. She is the right
mouth for it because she is *still bitter about being uniquely clumsy*: the world-
changing discovery happened because she, and only she, was careless enough to fall
in the dung. Everyone else, for three hundred years, had the discipline to stay
clean.

She pre-empts the question the reader is forming — *yes, obviously someone should have
found this sooner; the reason nobody did is that nobody was stupid enough to slip, and
the one who was is me, and you have named your unit of energy after my worst moment.*
That is the **soft lampshade**: a character saying the quiet part, not the narration
addressing the reader. The bitterness supplies all the wink the device needs; the
fictional membrane stays intact (see [[discovery-gating]] for why we landed here rather
than a literal fourth-wall break).

The line does triple duty: it answers the reader's objection, it characterises Dalla
(the only "achievement" of her life was an embarrassing accident, now monumentalised),
and it indicts the system (it built an economy on, and a unit of measure out of, one
woman's clumsiness — and calls it a founding).

> **Craft constraints:** hang the lampshade **once**, in Dalla's account, and don't let
> any other narrator re-explain the gap (a lampshade hung twice becomes an excuse). The
> [[the-guild|Guild]] should do the *opposite* — never admit the clumsiness, launder
> "she fell" into "she discovered." Dalla's bitter truth and the Guild's smooth myth
> are the same event, and the reader holds both.

## What she's structurally blind to
- The Dragon. She doesn't know the Dragon has an inner life, or that the system has
  done the same thing to both of them. Only the reader, holding both accounts, sees
  it.

## The unit/source rhyme (load-bearing)
Dalla and the Dragon are the two **female** characters, both rewarded for a form of
**bodily** compliance, both held in place not by force but by **incentive structure**:

- **Dalla** = *the unit*. Her body in the dung is the origin story; she's valued only
  as the legible face of the discovery.
- **Dragon** = *the source*. Her body as factory is the ongoing operation; valued
  only as long as her body stays in role.

Both occupy positions of apparent centrality that are actually positions of
**display**. "Face and source." For the formal rhyme: give Dalla's narration and the
Dragon's narration a **shared rhythm** — similar sentence shapes, similar bitter
asides, similar observations about being watched by people who think they own you.
Close enough that the reader *feels* the echo without being told.

## Voice notes
- Wry, compressed, allergic to the pieties spoken about her.
- The leering should be shown through her noticing of it, not narrated about her.

## Open questions
- How much of the true account ("I fell") does she let the reader have, and how
  early? *(The lampshade line probably wants to come fairly early in her chapter — it's
  the thesis statement of her bitterness — but the full "and they named the unit after
  it" turn can land later.)*
- Is the "Dalla" unit defined explicitly in the Guild chapter and felt obliquely in
  hers — or vice versa?
- Exact register of the lampshade line: closer to dry ("Someone had to be the fool;
  the post was vacant; I applied without meaning to") or to raw grievance? Her voice is
  *wry and compressed*, so probably the former — the grievance underneath, not on top.
