# Discovery-gating — "why now, and not centuries earlier?"

*The central flaw [[background|the brief]] flags: if guano's potency is revealed
just by **burning soiled clothes**, the discovery looks too inevitable — it should
have happened long ago. See [[genre-theory]] for the genre half and [[story-bible]]
for where the fix lands.*

---

## CANON (decided 2026-05-30): lampshade it — the fourth-wall break

**The chosen resolution does not explain the gap away. It names it.** A character asks
exactly what the reader is asking — *"why was this never discovered before?"* — and the
honest answer is anticlimactic and damning: on every previous raid, the whole
discipline was **go to the treasure, stay away from the dragon** — and so away from its
dung. **No one had ever been soiled this way before.** It is so obvious in retrospect
that it seems unbelievable — *but that's what happened.*

Why this is the right call for *this* story (not a cop-out):

- **It fits the register.** The piece is already ironic and [[the-guild|institutionally
  self-aware]]; a knowing meta-beat belongs here in a way a sober economic mechanism
  would not. The genre is satire, and satire is allowed to wink — once.
- **It pays off the accident.** The discovery *requires* [[dalla|Dalla's fall]] — the
  first time anyone broke the one rule everyone always kept. The lampshade retroactively
  makes her accident the *only* way the discovery could ever have happened. That is what
  turns her from a footnote into "the [[dalla|Dalla]]."
- **The chemistry stacks the contingencies even higher** (see [[guano]], the ignition
  rules). Fresh dung is wet and inert; it only ignites once **dried** — and only to
  human/dwarf fire. So the discovery needed *three* things to coincide: someone **soiled**
  (Dalla), the dung left to **dry** on the clothes, and those clothes meeting **fire**. Each
  alone is nothing; the stack is the whole industrial age. This makes "obvious in retrospect,
  unbelievable it took so long" *more* earned, not less — and it's all carried casually, by
  mechanism, never explained.
  - *(Optional fourth factor — the [[guano|catalyst]]: an abundant, mundane organic material
    in her clothes that the reaction also needs. Crucially **abundant**, so it does not turn
    the lampshade from "she fell in it" into "she had the magic ingredient" — and it's
    revealed only **retrospectively**, by the alchemists, never at the discovery moment. So
    the lampshade stays simple on the page; the catalyst is back-fill that deepens the
    chemistry without complicating the joke.)*
- **It rhymes structurally.** It echoes Scene 1's crew treating the treasure with
  indifference (the inversion already dramatised), and it sets up the
  [[the-guild|Guild's]] founder-myth, which will try to launder "a woman fell in it"
  into "a discovery." The reader gets the truth and the laundering both.
- **The obviousness is itself a theme.** That the world-changing fact sat in plain
  sight, guarded only by disgust and habit, *is* the [[critique-and-direction|determinist
  argument]] in miniature: the technology was always latent; only a contingent accident
  released it. The 4th-wall break smuggles the thesis in as a joke.

**Who delivers it — DECIDED: [[dalla|Dalla]]**, in her own account, *bitter about being
uniquely clumsy.* The discovery happened because she alone, in three hundred years, was
careless enough to fall in the dung; everyone else had the discipline to stay clean.
She pre-empts the reader's objection as grievance, not exposition — *"someone had to be
the fool; the post was vacant; I applied without meaning to."*

This also settles the **degree**: it is the **soft lampshade** (a character saying the
quiet part out loud), **not** a literal fourth-wall break (the narration addressing the
reader). Dalla's bitterness supplies the whole wink while the fictional membrane stays
intact — which matters in a Rashomon structure that depends on fully-inhabited,
unreliable narrators; a true address-to-the-reader would risk puncturing that illusion.
The line should land **once**, in her account only; the [[the-guild|Guild]] does the
inverse and launders "she fell" into "she discovered." A lampshade hung twice becomes an
excuse. See [[dalla]].

> The material below is now **supporting texture, not the mechanism.** Keep it for
> backstory colour, for the [[the-guild|Guild's]] retroactive rationalisations, and in
> case the lampshade ever needs a half-serious underpinning. It is no longer the fix.

## Why the problem is real but solvable

The instinct that "someone would have noticed" assumes discovery is driven by
*availability* (the dung was always there). Economic history says discovery and
**adoption** are driven by *profitability and prerequisites* — which can be absent for
centuries while the raw material sits in plain sight. Four well-evidenced mechanisms,
any of which (or a combination) can gate the guano discovery credibly:

### A. Induced innovation / relative prices — *"it wouldn't have paid yet"*
A labour- or resource-saving technology appears only when relative factor prices make
it **profitable to adopt**. Robert Allen's blunt formulation of the spinning machine:
*"it would not have paid to use spinning machines before the eighteenth century —
hence, they were not invented earlier."* *(High confidence on the general mechanism;
the **specific** British high-wage/spinning case is actively disputed — Humphries &
Schneider, Stephenson, Mokyr, Kelly & Ó Gráda. Use the mechanism, not the British
particulars, as fact.)*

Corroborated for 1700–1914 Britain: *"labour-saving technology adoption was a major
response to changes in relative factor prices … induced innovation was a major driver
of technical change"* — the bias was specifically labour-saving, capital-deepening and
**energy-using**, enabled by cheap coal. *(High confidence — Otojanov, Fouquet &
Granville, *Economic History Review*, 2023.)*

> **In-world:** before the story, fuel and fertiliser were cheap/sufficient enough
> that nobody had a *reason* to assay dragon dung. A recent **scarcity shock** changes
> the relative price and suddenly makes the unpleasant, dangerous option pay.

### B. Path dependence — *"a chance early turn locked in the wrong incumbent"*
A technology's dominance can be set by *"temporally remote events, including
happen-stance dominated by chance,"* not by inherent superiority; such processes are
*non-ergodic* and *"do not converge automatically"* to the optimal outcome. A superior
alternative can be **delayed or never adopted**. *(High confidence — Paul David, "Clio
and the Economics of QWERTY," 1985. The *definitional possibility* is solid; whether
QWERTY itself was truly inferior is disputed by Liebowitz & Margolis — but the brief
only needs the possibility.)*

### C. Lock-in without conspiracy — *"the market actively suppressed it, rationally"*
Lock-in arises from three reinforcing forces — **technical interrelatedness** (system
compatibility), **economies of scale**, and **quasi-irreversibility of investment** —
and, crucially, *"decentralized decision making"* by myopic, profit-motivated, free-to-
choose agents can drive an industry *"prematurely into standardization on the wrong
system"* with **no irrationality, conspiracy, or state interference required.** *(High
confidence — David 1985.)*

> **In-world:** this lets the dragon-slaying economy (heroes' guilds, bounties,
> reliquary trade in dragon parts) be a *locked-in incumbent* that no individual had an
> incentive to defect from — until the novum makes defection pay. No villain needed;
> the *system* suppressed the discovery.

### D. Cultural / institutional taboo — *the disgust gate*
*(Requested but **not** evidenced in this research pass — flagged as an open question.
Treat as a narrative device with a plausible-but-uncited basis, not a researched fact.)*
The handling of dung/ordure as ritually unclean is a natural suppressor: a society can
*know* dung exists and still have nobody of the right standing willing to **assay** it.
Note this is also the engine of [[dalla|Dalla's]] whole arc — the discovery is
disgusting, which is why it takes an *accident* (a fall) rather than an experiment.

## The real-world rhyme: guano and saltpetre

These aren't proof, but they're the historical resonances the world is built on, and
they model the "potent substance hiding in waste" pattern precisely.

- **Peruvian guano boom (c.1840s–1870s).** Seabird excrement, accumulated over
  millennia on rainless islands, became a global fertiliser commodity once 19th-
  century European agriculture hit a **nitrogen bottleneck** on carrying capacity
  (the Malthusian crunch). Guano had been used by pre-Columbian Andean societies for
  centuries — so its value was *known locally* yet sat economically inert globally
  until **demand conditions** (depleted European soils + transport + a fertiliser
  market) made it pay. The boom then collapsed as deposits were exhausted and
  **Haber–Bosch** synthetic nitrogen-fixation (1900s–1910s) superseded it. *(Sources
  fetched but not formally verified in this pass — see list; the broad arc is
  well-established history.)*

  > **This is the master-template for the whole project:** a biological energy/fertility
  > source, seemingly an unlimited gift, whose success creates the conditions for its
  > own exhaustion and supersession. It maps onto both the **discovery gate** (demand-
  > driven) *and* the [[the-dragon|Dragon's]] decline arc (the source runs out / breaks
  > down). The guano name is not incidental — it's the thesis.

- **Saltpetre / niter-beds (gunpowder's nitrate).** Potassium nitrate — the
  indispensable oxidiser in gunpowder — was deliberately *cultivated* in **niter-beds**
  from dung, urine, nightsoil and ash, turning human/animal waste into military power.
  A "300-year industrial secret" pattern: a high-value substance literally farmed from
  ordure. *(Sources fetched, not formally verified.)*

  > **In-world:** saltpetre is the closer mechanical analogue to guano-as-explosive
  > (the [[the-king|throne-room coup]] demolition charge). It also proves the
  > disgust-gate (D) is *crossable*: societies absolutely will harvest power from waste
  > once the incentive is large enough.

## Storyboard: candidate in-world gates (SUPERSEDED — kept for reference)

*These were the "serious" fixes considered before the [[#CANON (decided 2026-05-30):
lampshade it — the fourth-wall break|fourth-wall lampshade]] was chosen. They are no
longer canon, but any of them can supply **diegetic colour** the lampshade gestures at
(e.g. the disgust-gate, D, is exactly the "stay away from the dung" habit the meta-line
names). The strongest of the serious options combined a scarcity shock breaking a
taboo-reinforced lock-in (A + C + D).*

1. **The husbandry prerequisite (enabling technology).** You cannot collect dragon
   dung at scale until dragons are *kept* rather than *slain* — and keeping dragons is
   itself the new thing. Before the story, every dragon was killed on sight (locked-in
   incumbent, mechanism C), so **dung never accumulated** in collectable quantity. The
   discovery requires a captive dragon, which requires someone to *not* kill one first
   — the accident that starts everything. *(Cleanest single fix: it's a prerequisite,
   not a coincidence.)*

2. **The scarcity trigger (induced innovation, A).** Stage a recent **fuel or soil
   crisis** (a fertility collapse, a charcoal/forest exhaustion, a cold decade) that
   makes any new energy source suddenly worth the disgust and danger. The novum doesn't
   arrive because someone got clever; it arrives because the *price finally moved*.

3. **The potency change (in-world, removes the "always inevitable" objection at the
   root).** The dung is only super-potent because of the **engineered feeding regime** —
   the same regime that later makes the [[the-dragon|Dragon metabolically ill]]. *Wild*
   dragon dung was unremarkable; *farmed* dragon dung is the fuel. This elegantly closes
   the loop: the thing that makes guano potent is the same thing that eventually breaks
   the source. The discovery genuinely **could not** have happened earlier because the
   substance **did not exist** in potent form earlier.

   > *Historical-note (pre-lampshade): this "husbandry creates the potency" spine was
   > the strongest serious fix and still makes a tidy loop with the Dragon's ending. If
   > the lampshade ever needs a half-serious underpinning, this is it — but it is not
   > canon.*
