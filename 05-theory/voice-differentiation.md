# Voice differentiation — the multi-axis register map

*A craft note (branch: second-iteration, 2026-06-10). Prompted by the GPT review §6: the
book risks sounding like "one very intelligent mind refracted into role, rather than nine
minds with different sentence instincts." The high/low register split (rev. 2026-05-31 —
[[grundrak|Grundrak]] and [[brask|Brask]] written "worse" on purpose) was the first axis.
This note maps the rest, because register elevation alone won't separate the four
narrators who still rhyme: the [[the-king|King]], [[vesser|Vesser]], the
[[the-dragon|Dragon]], and (residually) [[grundrak|Grundrak]] are all superb at
retrospective compression — image rising to thesis at roughly the same rate.*

---

## The axes

Elevation (high/low) is one dial. There are at least eight more, and most of the
remaining voice-collisions can be solved without touching elevation at all:

1. **Document type / occasion.** *What artefact is this chapter, in-world, and who is it
   for?* The most underused lever. Two chapters already mark it strongly — [[dalla|Dalla
   on-air]] (transcript) and [[the-guild|the Guild]] (address). Several others gesture at
   an occasion without committing: Marga answers a question someone has asked her
   (*"You want to know what it's actually like"* — who wants?); Grundrak is "setting this
   down early"; Brask says "this is meant to be a record" (commissioned by whom?); the
   Dragon speaks "only here, where it cannot be overheard." **Recommendation:** commit
   each account to a specific artefact and let the artefact's conventions do
   differentiation work the prose currently does alone. (See the dossier question in
   [[second-iteration-proposals]] — this axis and the temporal-anchor fix from the GPT
   review §1 can be solved by the same device.)

2. **Addressee.** To no one (Dalla-private) ≠ to posterity (King) ≠ to an asker (Marga)
   ≠ to a censor *and* a mother at once ([[the-bride|Liesse]]). The felt presence or
   absence of a listener changes every sentence.

3. **What the mind notices first.** Marga: weight, smell, ache. The King: counts, times,
   routes. The Princess: temperature and speed. Brask: mechanism. Garran: distance — who
   can reach whom (note he and the Dragon *share* this one deliberately; it's the book's
   buried rhyme, "the arithmetic of who can reach whom"). Vesser: his own sensations
   *as performances* ("I want that hour"). The Dragon: appetite and patience.

4. **Metaphor source-domain.** Assign each narrator a home domain and *forbid* trespass:
   - Grundrak — ore, load, assay, walls, curves.
   - Vesser — breath, water, tide, weather. (He owns the tide; nobody else may use it.)
   - King — masonry, ledgers, coinage, instruments of state.
   - Princess — travel, fabric, temperature, price.
   - Garran — range, lines, graves, arithmetic.
   - Marga — almost none; her comparisons are tools and furrows, and rare.
   - Brask — none at all; he describes mechanism literally (a metaphor would be a defect).
   - Dragon — hunting, weather-as-patience, taxonomy of food.
   - Physician (proposed) — none permitted on the record; one escapes into a margin.
   - Liesse (proposed) — proverbs of her own tongue, rendered literally, slightly wrong.

5. **Abstraction budget** — how often image is allowed to rise into thesis. This is where
   the four high narrators collide. Proposed rationing: **Vesser unlimited** (essayistic
   beauty is his *flaw*, confessed in-chapter: "this is the part of an elegy that lies");
   **King two or three**, each delivered as a forensic finding, not a meditation;
   **Dragon several, but every abstraction must pass through the body** (appetite,
   weight, taste) before it may generalise; **Grundrak one**, the chair line, already
   locked. **Marga: one per account, immediately deflated** ("a true thought and a
   useless one").

6. **Irony stance** — how willing the narrator is to state their own irony:
   - States it as analysis: King.
   - States it late, resentfully, as a finding forced on him: Grundrak.
   - States it then disowns it: Garran ("I am sure there was nothing to connect it to").
   - States it proudly, as health: Princess.
   - Performs it: Dalla on-air. Audits it: Dalla private.
   - Cannot see it: Brask (the reader supplies all of it).
   - Refuses it as useless: Marga.
   - States it as predation: Dragon.
   - Half-sees and re-buries it: the Physician (proposed) — his formula for this is the
     margin/record distinction.
   - Cannot afford it: Liesse (proposed) — the Censor strikes it; irony survives only in
     the gaps.

7. **Syntactic tic** — one signature move each, used often enough to be a fingerprint:
   - Grundrak: hammering triplets ("year on year on year"; "And again. And again.").
   - Vesser: self-interrupting qualifications ("— I am almost certain —").
   - King: the formal performative ("I want to record"; "I have it exactly").
   - Princess: the long sentence that arrives somewhere warm.
   - Garran: short declarative, then the longer sentence that takes it back.
   - Marga: the flat full stop where another voice would explain.
   - Brask: de-contracted statements; "I do not understand that. I have stopped
     expecting it to change."
   - Dalla-private: the joke, then the audit of the joke.
   - Dragon: the suspended period — clauses stacked with predatory patience before the
     strike.
   - Physician: recurring formulae ("The recommendation is renewed"; "X has a definition
     in my ledger").
   - Liesse: the over-correct formality of the careful second-language writer — never
     broken grammar; *too-perfect* grammar.

8. **Forbidden moves** — the cheapest enforcement mechanism. One per narrator:
   - King: no organic imagery (water, growth, breath — those are Vesser's and the
     Dragon's).
   - Vesser: no numbers. He never counts; the count was the thing that beat him.
   - Grundrak: no beauty except the one spike.
   - Princess: no grief. Not suppressed — absent.
   - Garran: no glory-vocabulary, even negated.
   - Marga: no similes longer than a clause.
   - Brask: no interiority verbs about others ("he felt", "she hoped" — he can't know).
   - Dragon: no pity, including self-pity.
   - Physician: no first-person feelings on the record (they leak into margins only).
   - Liesse: no direct accusation (the form forbids it; the strikes carry it).

## Why "write worse" worked — and its limit

The 2026-05-31 re-voicing succeeded because it changed *competence texture*, not just
diction: Grundrak's roughness reads as a man who thinks in loads and doesn't revise;
Brask's flatness reads as a man with no audience-model. The limit: "worse" only separates
the low-register family. The high-register family (King / Vesser / Dragon) can't be
written worse — they must be separated by **what kind of excellent** they are: the King's
excellence is *forensic*, Vesser's is *musical*, the Dragon's is *predatory*. Same
altitude, different animals.

## Audit checklist (for the revision pass)

Per chapter: (1) name the artefact and addressee in one line; (2) count the
image→thesis conversions against the budget above; (3) check the metaphor domain for
trespass; (4) confirm the tic appears ≥2 times and the forbidden move 0 times; (5) read
one paragraph aloud against the two nearest-register neighbours — if it could be
re-attributed, mark it.
