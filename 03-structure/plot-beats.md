## Ordering logic — the first-iteration draft order

The accounts are **not** chronological; each enters the timeline where its own arc
peaks. The order as drafted (2026-05-30):

- **I · [[marga|Marga]]** — **opens the book.** Her account now *contains* the descent
  (formerly the standalone prologue), retold in her first person, then sets the *drudgery of the
  new normal* as the ground truth the later self-dramatisations are measured against. The un-genre
  first inoculates the reader against the genres to come.
- **II · [[grundrak|Grundrak]]** + **III · [[vesser|Vesser]] (the elegy)** — the trials
  told twice, opposite arc positions, shared concrete detail. Teaches the book's method:
  events fixed, meanings not.
- **IV · [[dalla|Dalla]] (on air)** — pivots from the system's *makers* to one of its

- **V · [[the-king|The King]]** — hard power and the coup.
- **VI · [[the-princess|The Princess]]** — the same marriage from *inside* the new order;

- **VII · [[vesser|Vesser]] (watching [[brask|Brask]])** — the magecraft pair's second
  half; the mourner narrates the Hum's builder.
- **VIII · [[garran|Garran]]** — the third displaced incumbent; the Hummingbird demonstration.
- **IX · [[dalla|Dalla]] (private)** — the weary monologue that dismantles the on-air

## The two organising rhymes

### 1. The unit and the source (the working subtitle)
Dalla and the Dragon — both female, both rewarded for **bodily** compliance, both
caged by **incentive** rather than force. Dalla is *the unit* (a person become a
measurement); the Dragon is *the source* (a body become a factory). Make the rhyme
**formal**: shared sentence-rhythms and "being watched by people who think they own
you" asides across the two chapters. Neither knows the other; only the reader holds
both.

### 2. The taxonomy of submission
Three characters, three mechanisms, one system:
- **The King — hard power.** Walls broken; arithmetic done.
- **Dalla — soft power / incentive.** Status that entombs.
- **The Dragon — soft power / incentive.** Comfort that contains.
Hard power (the King) rhymes against soft power (Dalla, the Dragon). The system
doesn't need to choose one mode; it uses whichever fits the subject.

> **Scaled up, this is the whole order's structure** (see [[labour-and-order]]): a
> **monopoly of violence** with two faces — a **fist** (guano firepower, the
> [[story-bible|Hummingbirds]], the [[garran|Peacekeepers]]) and a **voice** (the
> [[the-hum|Hum]], [[dalla|Dalla's]] celebrity, the [[the-guild|Guild's]] myth). Hard power
> and soft power aren't just two characters' fates — they're the two arms of the Leviathan,
> and the hard/soft-magic axis runs right through the politics.

### 3. The taxonomy of submission, extended — where the Princess fits
The King / Dalla / Dragon triad is *submission*. The [[the-princess|Princess]] is the
fourth term the system produces once submission is complete: **assimilation**. She
isn't broken (hard power) or bought (soft power); she is *raised inside* the new order
and never knew the old one as home. If the others show the system taking power, she
shows it reproducing itself — the most durable victory of all. Decide whether she joins
this rhyme or stands deliberately outside it.

Its physical emblem: the [[story-bible|too-low dwarven tunnels]] that make humans
**bow**. The King would feel that stoop as humiliation; the Princess no longer feels it
at all, and hopes her children are *born* short enough not to. Submission (the King) →
assimilation (the Princess) is the whole conquest compressed into the question of how
tall you are allowed to stand.

### 4. The displaced incumbents vs. the ascendant generation
The novum sorts the cast into two cohorts, and the book pairs them off:

| Old-world vocation rendered obsolete | Ascendant new-world counterpart |
|---|---|
| [[the-king\|King]] — sovereignty | [[grundrak\|Grundrak]] — real power behind the throne |
| [[vesser\|Vesser]] — magecraft | [[brask\|Brask]] — the Hum's builder |
| [[garran\|Garran]] — heroism / arms | *(the Hummingbirds themselves — no human heir)* |

The first two pairs have a human on each side; the third is the darkest, because the
slayer's successor is **not a person but a machine** ([[story-bible|Hummingbirds]]). Heroism doesn't get a
proud young inheritor the way sovereignty and magecraft do — it just gets *automated
out*. **The Hummingbird is also the hinge between [[garran|Garran's]] arc and the
[[dragons|dragons']] ending:** the same swarm that ends the slayer's role would be the
first thing able to kill a dragon, which is the cold trigger for the dragons leaving. One
technology displaces the old killer *and* spooks the apex predator — the reader connects
the two; neither party does. Note too that [[vesser|Vesser]] and [[garran|Garran]] share the **self-flattering
elegy** blindness: each mourns a vocation (artisanal magic / "honest" violence) that the
determinist reading says was always doomed or always a lie. Don't let them sound alike —
Vesser is lyrical, Garran is flat and guilt-shadowed — but they rhyme.

### 5. The generational triad (old remembers, young is at home)
A related but distinct pattern — *generational assimilation*, the old-world figure who
**remembers** set against the young one who finds the new order *home*:

| Remembers the old world | Born to the new |
|---|---|
| [[the-king\|King]] | [[the-princess\|Princess]] |
| [[vesser\|Vesser]] | [[brask\|Brask]] |
| [[the-dragon\|The Dragon]] | younger dragons (the golden cages feel like home) |

Three iterations make it a deliberate motif, not a coincidence — and the **third
*breaks* the pattern** (chosen lean: see [[dragons]]). Alone among the "old" figures the
[[the-dragon|Dragon]] has **longevity** (she outlives everyone) and access to a channel
humans can't hear (the [[above-and-below|Hum-silent deep]]), so she carries the memory of
wildness *to* the young instead of being replaced by them — **assimilation running
backwards.** This is the one inversion in the whole ensemble, and it's why the dragons get
the last word.

## Determinism preserved
Across all accounts, the technology still wins. The disagreement is never about *what
happened* — it's about meaning, blame, dignity, and cost. That is the whole design:
system-level determinism, interpretation-level chaos.

> **The dragons act (chosen lean — [[dragons]] option C):** it must **not** soften
> determinism. Frame the coordination/defection as the **mispriced foundation reasserting
> itself mechanically** — the externality (the dragons' inner lives) coming due — *not* as
> heroic free choice. Determinism doesn't only mean "the tech wins"; it means a system that
> misprices its own base is **determined to fail.** Read that way, the collapse turns the
> iron law on the *winners* and *strengthens* the thesis rather than breaking it. Keep it
> cold, embodied, ambiguous (the humans stay oblivious) — never Spartacus, never
> Boxer-on-the-nose.

## Per-chapter scaffolding (Story Circle)
Run a small Harmon circle inside each account (you / need / go / search / find /
take / return / change). The point is that a **single shared external beat lands at
a different circle-position for each narrator** — climax for Grundrak, inciting loss
for Vesser. Track each character's circle in their character file as you draft.

## Watch-list (failure modes from the prior drafts)
- **The Boxer problem.** No on-the-nose allegory flags. The fossil-fuel and
  industrial-revolution readings must stay *carried by mechanism*, never stated.
- **Inert glue.** The Arrath Keep siege was cut for being narrative glue. Audit every
  new scene: does it do character *and* structural work, or is it just transit?
- **Agreement.** If two narrators agree about what an event *meant*, one of them is
  redundant. Force the disagreement.
