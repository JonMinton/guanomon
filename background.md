The purpose of this project is to evolve and codevelop an existing coproduced story in a way that foregrounds individual character experiences by using a Rashomon-style device (i.e. being written mainly/entirely from the perspective of different characters, one chapter per character). 

The story is/was called The Guano Guild. The underlying conceit is that of taking the classic Sci-Fi concept of the Novum and applying it to a Tolkienesque world, in which there is arguably less focus on technology and more on maintaining a bucolic status quo. The Novum in this case is an accidental discovery of huge potency in dragon dung (called Guano to rhyme with the discovery that some bird feces were very potent sources of nitrogen and helped crops grow, ultimately raising the carrying capacity for humans). 

There are some existing version of the story, which were (rightly) categorised as too distant/functionalist. These are available on my blog [one level up, then into quarto-blogs/jon-blog/ or similar: please add link, either to that or github]. 

A social revolution occurs as a result of the dragon guano discovery. This broadly follows the transition of wealth and power from inherited wealth (the old rich) to industrial wealth (the new rich) which occurred in the UK from (say) around the 1700s to 1900s (then perhaps accelerating further in the World Wars). This is represented by the defacto transfer of power from The King to The Dwarf. 

A number of notes from earlier chats are available in files.zip. Please unpack these in a sensible way. 

I am considering using obsidian to create a network of files/notes which are mutually intelligible/parseable to humans and LLMs. 

Please do not see the density of existing notes as an invitation to start writing the story itself too early. Instead let's spend some time developing the story and underlying ideas carefully. This might, for instance, involve searching for and pulling materials on the concept of Novum, the differences between sci fi and fantasy, and the hard/soft magic distinction which arguably forms a link between the two. 

One particular flaw in the central conceit is that, if the discovery emerge simply by burning soiled clothes, then maybe it was too inevitable. Some means of explaining away why it happens during the story's timeline and not before is therefore worth storyboarding. 

A nice-to-have will be to consider how to deploy and design a github page site for the story itself. This might involve, for instance, elaborate cursive on the first character of each chapter.
