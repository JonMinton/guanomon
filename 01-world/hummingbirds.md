# Hummingbirds — the fused novum

> A world-entity note *(added on branch second-iteration, 2026-06-10 — consolidates material
> previously spread across [[story-bible]], [[the-hum]], [[dragons]] and [[guilds]]; those
> remain authoritative where they conflict).* The Hummingbird is the point where the
> [[guano|hard novum]] and the [[the-hum|soft novum]] stop being separate machines and
> become **one weapon**: guano ordnance, guided by the Hum. Small, flying, remotely
> directed. *Boxer guard: never say "drone." The Hummingbird carries it.*

## What it is, mechanically (keep it Sanderson-legible)
[[brask|Brask's]] fork-guidance (the Hum turned from *carrying* a voice to *steering* a
thing) married to [[korrin|Korrin's]] controlled fuel (the [[guano|dial]] between slow-burn
and blast). A singing-engine "barely a single reed" rides in the breast; the device
*listens* — to a few controlling forks, and to the devices nearest it on a **graded
pitch-ladder** — and out of that small minding "a large coordinated motion comes, with
almost no one directing it." Brask thinks it the best result of his life.

**The murmuration irony (locked, rev. 2026-05-31):** the swarm-coordination is,
unknowingly, the dragons' own **[[the-hum|murmur]]** principle — leaderless coordination
out of mutual listening — reinvented above ground and fitted to a weapon. He weaponises
the very pattern that will slip the leash below. Neither he nor anyone human ever
recognises the symmetry; the reader does.

## Naming
"Hummingbird" is the *poetic* coinage in a world of [[the-hum|dull names]] — of the Hum,
and a small bright lethal flying thing. Per [[above-and-below]] (naming subnote), the
lyricism marks it as an **above-ground, human coinage** — the dwarves would have called it
a *steerbird* or worse. Brask: "a soft word for a clean piece of work."

## Status & credit
Experimental at the story's late beats; demonstrations real enough to frighten
([[garran|Garran's]] yard). A **joint guild project** — Sappers' (fuel) + Hummers' (signal)
— credited institutionally to "the guilds," no individual named ([[guilds]]). Brask and
Korrin are the uncredited co-designers; Brask cannot make himself mind.

## Who it threatens (one trajectory, two displaced readers)
- **[[garran|Garran]]** — consciously, mournfully: force-projection abroad is the slayer's
  *last* role, and the swarm has "started on the hand." Heroism's heir is not a person.
- **The [[dragons]]** — coldly, and acting on it: guano-powered, the Hummingbird can never
  replace the source, but a mature swarm is **the first thing able to coerce or kill a
  dragon** — the soft cage acquiring the power to become hard, "closed without setting a
  single bar." This is the impetus for the leaving; dragon-paced (5+ years, extrapolation
  not crisis). Full logic: [[dragons]] §"Why now."

## Expansion question — answered (lean)
Should the Hummingbird *programme* grow (a Hummer-operator POV, a strike abroad shown,
a malfunction)? **Lean: no.** Its power is that it never finishes arriving: every account
sees the trajectory, none sees the arrival; the reader closes the loop. The one expansion
worth considering is **negative space** — the Guild address already promises "more sparing
of our own people," and the next iteration of the address (never written) would have to
name what the swarm did. That absence can stay absent.
