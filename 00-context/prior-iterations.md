# Prior iterations — carried over from the development chat

> **Status: reconstructed from the chat, not canonical.** Scene 1 below is close
> to the text drafted in the conversation. The later accounts exist here as
> *design summaries* because the chat preserved their structure and intent more
> fully than their finished prose. Replace these with your authoritative local /

The story passed through three linear drafts before the Rashomon pivot. The
progression added, in order: a split discovery scene introducing Grundrak (dwarf,
applied empiricist) against Vesser (mage, motivated resistance); a mage-vs-guano
trials scene; a normalisation montage; a first-person dragon narrator as
retrospective reframe; then plumbing infrastructure, an etymologically grounded
urine-tasting diagnosis, reduced crew scheduling as a countdown device, and dragon-
urine luxury confectionery as a class detail. The on-the-nose *Animal Farm* / Boxer
reference was flagged for removal.

## Scene 1: The Descent

*[Cold open. Audience joins mid-circle. All beats implied, none shown. The scene's
job is disorientation through genre subversion.]*

The torchlight caught the edge of Marga's shovel as she drove it into the mound.
The smell was beyond description — beyond even memory, because the nose learned to
forget between shifts, and each return to the deep chamber was a fresh education in
revulsion.

"Steady," whispered Aldric from behind her. "She's sleeping left-side. Stay right
and we'll fill eight bags before the turn of the hour."

They moved in practised formation: Marga shovelling, Kael holding the waxed sacks
open, Aldric watching the dragon's flank for any change in the rhythm of its
breathing. The creature lay curled around a hillock of gold coins and gemstones
that glittered obscenely in the torchlight. None of them spared it a glance.

*[The treasure is visible but irrelevant. No character acknowledges it. The
audience's expectation — that the gold is the objective — is violated by
indifference rather than by exposition.]*

Kael tied off the sixth sack and winced as the knot squelched under his fingers.
"Lovely," he murmured. "Another day in paradise."

"Quiet," said Aldric. Not unkindly. They had all said worse.

The dragon's breath hitched. All three froze. A long, rattling exhale, and the
flank settled again. Marga resumed shovelling.

By the time they emerged into grey daylight, the cart was heavy and the smell had
become, as it always did, a kind of nothing — a thing so constant it erased itself.
Kael drove. Marga sat on the back with her boots hanging off the edge, watching the
lair entrance shrink behind them.

"Same time Thursday?" said Aldric.

"Same time Thursday."

## Scene 2: The Fall *(discovery + schism)*

*[First of two linked Story-Circle beats. The accident and the schism: the party
discovers guano's potency but divides over its meaning. The circle completes for
the group but forks into two competing interpretations — Grundrak's and Vesser's.]*

Set six months before Scene 1. A conventional adventuring party with conventional
expectations descends on the lair. The discovery is accidental (this is the seed of
Dalla's whole arc — see her character file). Grundrak reads the residue as an
*ore nobody has thought to assay*; Vesser reads it as a threat to the value of
magecraft. The connection for the dwarf is professional, not intellectual — dwarves
are already the archetype of extraction, processing, combustion.

## The trials *(Grundrak's vindication)*

Mage output is set against guano output. The mage *can* do what guano does, but at
enormous personal cost and lower throughput: artisanal versus industrial, not true
versus false. The mage is a handloom weaver watching the power loom arrive. Ends
with Vesser's capitulation.

## Normalisation montage

The institutional/Guild voice smooths the preceding conflict into a tidy progress
narrative. Infrastructure appears: plumbing reinforced around the dragon (noticed by
the dragon in passing, without understanding the implication of its own weight gain),
reduced collection scheduling, the spread of guano through society. Mages migrate
from denying guano's superiority to trying to *reproduce* it as alchemists — the
Kodak-invents-digital-then-shelves-it trajectory.

## The throne-room coup *(replaces the Arrath Keep siege)*

Grundrak applies guano not to a foreign enemy but to his own king. He detonates the
palace walls at night and is sitting on the throne before the king has finished
dressing. The earlier siege of Arrath Keep (selling guano to Lord Catteral, walls
falling) is *retired* and reframed in implication as the rehearsal — proof of
concept before production deployment. Told **only** from the King's perspective
(Grundrak has already been humanised in his own account and works better unseen).
Key line: *"You didn't come to the demonstration, so I decided to bring the
demonstration to you."*

## A decade on *(colonial expansion)*

King a decade older, Grundrak *at his side* (not behind him). Mages reduced to
intelligence analysts supplying reports on neighbouring territories' resources —
dragons, fertile land, gem deposits — to decide where to "demonstrate" next. A
strategic marriage is arranged between Grundrak's child and the king's; the king
still harbours discriminatory attitudes toward dwarves, while his own child's
deference to the new hierarchy is already naturalised.

## The Dragon's account *(retrospective, outermost frame)*

The view from outside all human categories. Soft containment; thirty years of
comfort producing metabolic decline (diabetes — embodied dissatisfaction, not
ennui). The feeding regime made her sick, and sickness redirects appetite. The gaze
turns predatory again. Returning to hunting humans is framed, unresolved, as the
dragon's planned "get-fit routine" — whether she seeks the challenge of the old ways
or stays in her comfort zone is left open. (Fossil-fuel allegory sits underneath:
the obesity is the unpriced externality. The mechanism carries it; never stated.)
