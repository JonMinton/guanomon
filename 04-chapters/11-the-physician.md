# The Physician

---

> *Extracts from the case-book of the Physician-in-Ordinary to the Source, the eighteenth to the thirty-first years of the keeping. The case-book is the property of the Guild.*

**Eighteenth year of the keeping; second quarter.**

The office I hold was the crown's once. Physician-in-ordinary: taster to the royal body, keeper of its waters. My grandmother held the post, and her father before her, back along the whole line of kings. The art is unchanged — the cup, the finger, the tongue, the ledger. The body that matters has moved, and the office moved with it, downward. The patient is larger.

My warrant names two duties: the health of the source, and the steadiness of its yield. No provision is made for any case in which the two part company. I have not asked for one. The warrant is renewed yearly, at the Guild's pleasure.

Routine attendance. The sample was taken at the usual hour, from the usual channel. Sweet — and sweeter than the spring quarter, which was itself sweeter than the winter. I have entered *mellituria*, early presentation, and recommended a reduction of feed.

The recommendation goes to the committee. *(Appended, fourth quarter: the committee thanks me for my diligence. The schedule is unchanged. Output considerations are cited.)*

**Nineteenth year; first quarter.**

The cradle-grating beneath the patient has been reinforced a second time. The works were carried out at night, which is a courtesy to the patient, or to the workers; I did not ask which. Weight cannot be taken directly at this scale; I estimate from the displacement of the bedding-stone, and the estimate is not good news, and is filed where estimates are filed.

The keeper has begun to use the words *portion control* in the chamber, in the bright slow voice the keepers keep for the animals. I have asked that feed not be discussed within the patient's hearing. The keepers ask me why. I find I do not have an answer I am willing to put in a ledger, so I will put down the observable instead: she watches us. She has always watched us. It is recorded, in every one of my predecessors' hands, as docility.

**Twenty-first year; third quarter.**

I have this quarter compiled the returns from the other sites, which is not strictly my office, but it is no one else's either. The sweetness is not hers alone. Four of the younger animals show it — animals in their first decades of keeping, fed on the same schedule, presenting earlier in the keeping than she did. I have tabulated the onsets against the years under feed, and the table makes one shape, and I have sent the table to the committee with the shape marked.

*(Appended: the committee finds the apparent rise to be an artefact of improved measurement; our instruments are more sensitive than our predecessors' were. The committee's view is recorded in the column kept for views.)*

**Twenty-third year; second quarter.**

An incident at the northern site, which I set down because the case-book is where such things are set down, and because it is set down nowhere else that I know of.

A feeder was lost. The animal concerned — cave-born, young, with no record of prior distress — refused all feed thereafter, for three days. Restraint was achieved with the assistance of two senior animals, whose intervention was unprompted, and which the keepers note with admiration: the herd, they say, tends its own. The pinions were reduced at the joint, to prevent self-injury. Supported feeding was instituted by tube, and continues at this writing. The animal is calm.

Calm has a definition in my ledger. It accepts the pipe.

There is provision now for such cases — the convalescent caves, where the ones who stop eating are tended until they begin again. The keepers' word for the place means *rest*. I have attended once, in a consulting capacity. It is very quiet there.

**Twenty-fifth year; first quarter.**

The separation of clutches is now general policy across the sites, on welfare grounds: the dams' distress indices fall markedly once the young are removed to the nursery caves, and the young, raised to the hand, take to the schedule without the difficulties the wild-recruited generation gave us. The dams call for some weeks, and then stop. The cessation is recorded as settling.

I record it as settling. There is no other column.

**Twenty-sixth year; first quarter.**

A note on comprehension, which I set down because it troubles me and there is nowhere else to set it.

The patient anticipates. She is at her station before the carts are in the passage. She turns the printed face of the day-board toward herself — she, who can have no use for printing. And twice now, when two keepers have disputed a date within her hearing, her eye has gone to the one who proved right *before* the matter was settled between them. The wild-caught never did these things. The committee records it as successful gentling, and I have written, in the column provided, *conditioned to routine*.

She has never made a sound any of us could call a word; the gift, if it is one, runs only the one way. But conditioning does not turn a page toward itself. I will not write that twice. The margin may hold it the once: she reads us. She has read us all along, and we have spent thirty years settling her feed across her open ear, in a tongue we never once thought to ask whether she had learned.

**Twenty-seventh year; fourth quarter.**

The source's requisitions continue, and grow more particular — a cup bound with one kind of wire and not another; a reliquary set with the named crystal only; each indulged, enjoyed a while, and returned to the hoard when she tires of it. The committee is charmed. The indulgence costs nothing we still count. For my part I have scored the behaviour highly, under *engagement*: an animal that takes an interest is a well animal. It is the one appetite of hers the schedule did not make, and the only one we can afford to feed.

The returns this year note singing at three of the sites — seasonal, faint, low in the rock. Contented animals sing. It is filed under wellbeing.

**Twenty-ninth year; third quarter.**

Two men of the new fellowships attended the chamber this quarter, with the committee's leave and against my written advice, to conduct what their paper calls a stimulus trial: whether the patient retains the predatory response. A device was flown — small, very quick, with one of the singing-engines in its breast — across the breadth of the chamber and into a straw figure made in the shape of a man, the old strata faces having first been sheeted and the floor wetted, which is the one part of my advice that was taken. The figure did not survive it. Throughout: pupil unchanged. Posture unchanged. Respiration unchanged. Response: none observed.

Conclusion of the visitors, filed with the committee: the patient is old; the old hunger has gone out of her; the new instruments need take no account of the herd. I concurred in the report.

I add here, in the margin, where conclusions are not required: I have attended this animal for the better part of thirty years, and I have never once known her to show us anything she did not choose to show. The margin is not the record.

**Thirty-first year; second quarter.**

The singing is continuous now. Every site reports it — low, more in the rock than in the air, felt in the boot-soles sooner than heard, the harmonics layered past anything our instruments resolve. It does not stop, and no single animal can be identified as its source. The welfare committee has this year adopted song-frequency as its leading index of contentment, and by that index the herd has never been so well in all the years of the keeping. It is the only measure in my book that improves without any intervention of ours.

I have wondered, once, what it would cost us if we are wrong about what the singing measures. I did not pursue the thought.

The sweetness rises. The recommendation is renewed. The patient sings.
