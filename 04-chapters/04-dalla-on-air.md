# Dalla — On Air

---

> *Transcribed from the Hour. Seventh bell, in the twenty-fourth year of the keeping. The mark on the spool reads: a national treasure, returning.*

**SENNET.** — and she needs no further announcing, which has never once stopped me announcing her. Friends, you know the voice. You have known it longer than you have known some of your own family, and I will wager it has been kinder to you. Will you welcome, to the Hour, the woman the whole age is built upon — Dalla.

*[AUDIENCE: sustained applause]*

**DALLA.** You'll turn my head, Sennet.

**SENNET.** I should like to see it turned. I have been trying for a decade.

*[AUDIENCE LAUGHTER]*

**DALLA.** A decade. Don't.

**SENNET.** A *good* decade. Now — and I hope you won't mind my asking this, because I have wanted to ask it properly for years and never quite dared — when you lie awake, as we all do, in the small hours, and you think back to that day, the day that — forgive me — *began* all of this. The very first moment. What is it you see?

**DALLA.** Truthfully?

**SENNET.** Always truthfully. That is the whole of why they love you.

**DALLA.** I see the floor coming up very fast.

*[AUDIENCE LAUGHTER]*

**SENNET.** *[over the laughter]* No — no, this is what I mean, you see, friends, this is the *candour* of her —

**DALLA.** It's not candour, Sennet, it's a fall. I fell. People want it to be a moment of — I don't know — vision. A light on the road. There was no light. There was a ledge, and there was me, and there was a very great deal of what we had all spent three hundred years being far too sensible to go anywhere near, and I went into it head first because I have never in my life put my feet where I meant to.

*[AUDIENCE LAUGHTER]*

**SENNET.** And the world changed.

**DALLA.** The world changed. I changed my clothes. *[laughter]* I want that order kept, because everyone else reverses it.

**SENNET.** *[a performed pause]* You know, there's a thing I find — and tell me if I'm being foolish — there's something almost *sacred* in that, isn't there. That it should have come not from the wise, not from the mighty, but from — and I say this with the deepest affection —

**DALLA.** From a clumsy woman.

**SENNET.** From a — I would never —

**DALLA.** You'd never, but I would, and I'm the only one allowed, so let me. Somebody had to be the fool, Sennet. That's the part nobody likes. For three hundred years the rule was *go to the gold, stay out of the muck,* and every single soul who ever went down there kept the rule, because they weren't fools. And then there was one who didn't keep it, who couldn't keep her feet for one afternoon, and that's the entire miracle, start to finish. The post of fool was vacant. I applied without meaning to. *[she lets it sit]* And they have named a *unit of measurement* after me.

*[AUDIENCE LAUGHTER — and applause]*

**SENNET.** *[warmly]* They have. They have indeed. A man came to my own house last winter to read the meter, and there it was on his ledger, plain as morning — *so many of you* drawn, last quarter, by the house of Sennet. I confess I felt I was being charged for the pleasure of your company.

*[AUDIENCE LAUGHTER]*

**DALLA.** You were. I get nothing.

*[AUDIENCE LAUGHTER]*

**SENNET.** *[laughing]* She's incorrigible. Friends, she's incorrigible. — But seriously now. Seriously. Does it ever weigh on you? To be — and I choose the word with care — to be a *word*. To be the thing people *say* without thinking of you at all.

**DALLA.** *[a small beat]* Does it weigh on me.

**SENNET.** If it's too much —

**DALLA.** No. No, it's a fair question, it's the fairest question you've asked me, you sly old thing, you've been saving it. *[laughter]* — Honestly? You can't feel the weight of a thing that big. It's like asking does the sky weigh on you. It's just the air now. People draw a Dalla, they pay for a Dalla, they don't think of a person any more than they think of a person when they say a *pound* or a *mile*. And that's — *[she smiles; the smile is audible]* — that's restful, in a way. That's the only privacy I've got left. Nobody looks for the woman inside the unit.

**SENNET.** *[gently]* I look.

**DALLA.** *[lightly]* You do not, Sennet. You'd never find anywhere to put me. — Ask me something funny.

**SENNET.** *[the warmth, recovering itself]* Tell them about the hat.

**DALLA.** Oh, the *hat* —

*[AUDIENCE: anticipatory laughter]*

**DALLA.** — so. There is a town. I won't name it, they've suffered enough. And the mayor of this town has had made, in my honour, at I dread to think what cost —

**SENNET.** Tell them. Tell them the cost.

**DALLA.** I will not, it's obscene. A hat. A *ceremonial* hat, that I am to wear, on a stage, while he reads out a — there's a scroll, Sennet, there's a scroll —

*[AUDIENCE LAUGHTER, building]*

**DALLA.** — and the hat is shaped, in tribute, in *loving tribute*, like the ledge I fell off.

*[AUDIENCE: laughter and applause]*

**SENNET.** *[delighted, over it]* No.

**DALLA.** They made me a hat of the worst day of my life and I wore it and I smiled and I thanked him and it is, I should say, a *very good hat.*

*[AUDIENCE LAUGHTER]*

**SENNET.** *[as it dies down]* Dalla. As ever. As always. The woman the whole age is built upon — and still the funniest person I know. We'll have her back, friends. We always have her back. — Stay with us; after the bell, the weather over the provinces, and a word from the Guild.

*[AUDIENCE: applause]*
