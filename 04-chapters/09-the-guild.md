# Account the Ninth — The Guild

*Corporate annual report. The penultimate flattening: every cost above, laundered in one official voice.*

---

> *Being the Address of the Guild on the Occasion of its Anniversary, as hummed across the provinces at the appointed hour.*

It has been another year of progress, and it is right that we pause to measure it.

A generation ago this was a poorer, darker, and more dangerous world. Let the record show the distance travelled. Where once the night was lit only by those who could afford to waste tallow, the meanest household now draws light and warmth at a price that falls each year. Where once a journey across the city consumed a morning, it is accomplished, below, in comfort, in minutes. The figures are set out in the appended tables and they admit of only one reading. Adoption is universal. Output rises. The cost of energy, expressed in the standard unit, has never been lower. A grateful age may be forgiven for no longer remarking on marvels it has come to expect as its due.

Founding is a thing best remembered plainly. The discovery that transformed our fortunes is sometimes told as a matter of chance; the Guild prefers the truer telling. A discovery is not the accident that precedes it but the *understanding* that follows, and the understanding was ours: the assay, the trials, the patient characterisation of the substance and its properties, the long disciplined work by which a curiosity became an industry. We honour, of course, the individual with whose name the standard unit is associated; her place in the public affection is secure and well deserved. But sentiment should not crowd out fact. What was found that day was a possibility. What was built in the years since was a civilisation. The Guild built it.

The transition of the mages' ancient profession deserves particular notice, as a model of managed change. Where a lesser order might have resisted the future, that profession was guided, with the Guild's support, into new and productive channels — the careful study of the fuel, and latterly the development of the communication apparatus by which this very address reaches you. No tradition was lost. Every tradition was *repurposed*. We count this among our proudest achievements: that the old skills were not discarded but invited, gently, to become useful.

On the matter of public safety, the Guild speaks with confidence and with figures. It is the plain and verifiable truth that fewer of our people die by violence today than in any year our grandfathers knew. The old wars — the contested, ruinous wars of succession and border and pride that once emptied a generation of its young men — have simply ceased. The settlement of authority has been orderly and is now complete. Abroad, the projection of our methods has been efficient and, by every measure that can be kept, sparing of our own. At home, order is maintained. The numbers are not in dispute. Mortality, taken across the whole of society, has fallen and continues to fall. We say to any who would trouble the public mind with anxious talk: consult the total. The total has never been kinder.

It would be incomplete not to acknowledge that an economy of this scale generates, as all honest labour does, its share of incident. Work near energy carries hazard; this has been true since the first man tamed fire, and the Guild's safety provisions are kept under continuous review. New sectors have arisen — the agricultural enterprises that supply the necessary inputs chief among them — bringing employment to many and, with employment, the ordinary risks attendant on any vigorous trade. These are managed. They are, in the proper context, exceptional. They are not, and should not be made into, the story. The story is the light in the window and the warmth in the hearth and the journey made in minutes, and the long peace, and the falling total.

As to the foundation of it all, the Guild is pleased to report that the source remains well cared for. The herd is larger than at any time in our history and continues to grow; the animals are housed, fed, and tended at considerable expense, their conditions superior in every respect to anything nature afforded them. Observers report them docile and content; their keepers describe them, often, as *singing*. We find no cause for concern in any quarter, and every cause for confidence. A foundation this broad, this well-managed, and this thoroughly understood does not fail.

We end where we began, with measurement, because measurement is the Guild's first principle and its final defence. Sentiment can be argued. Memory can be coloured. But a figure in a column is a figure in a column, and ours are good, and they improve, and they will continue to improve, world without end.

It has been another year of progress.

It is right that we pause to measure it.

We have measured it.

The age is sound.
