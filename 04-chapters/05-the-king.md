# The King

---

I have set the night down so many times that I no longer trust my memory of it, only my record. So I will give you the record. It is precise. Precision is the one sovereignty they left me.

The first charge came at the third hour after midnight, against the outer curtain wall, on the northeast face, forty feet of granite that had stood through two sieges and a bad century. I did not yet know it was a charge. I knew it as a sound that should not exist — a sound with no army in front of it, no engines, no months of sapping, no warning from any watch. One sound, and then a silence in which forty feet of granite was simply no longer a fact about the world.

The second came against the inner gate at the fourth hour. The third against the doors of the keep itself. I have since had it explained to me, courteously, how little of the substance each one required. A man can carry a coup in a satchel now. I had spent my whole reign defending a perimeter that had ceased, without my being told, to mean anything at all. A wall is a sentence in an argument about who can reach whom. They had stopped arguing.

I want to record what I did, because it is the only thing in the whole account that was mine. I dressed.

I could hear them in the lower halls by then — not many of them, that is the part the histories enlarge; it did not take many — and I understood with complete clarity that there was no door between me and the throne room that would still be a door by the time I reached it. And I called for my man, and I dressed, fully, correctly, in the order it is done, every clasp. It was vanity. I will not pretend it was not vanity. But it was also the last act of sovereignty available to me, performed knowingly, performed for no audience, performed *because* it would change nothing — which is, I have come to think, the truest definition of a king: the one man whose ceremonies are obeyed by no one but himself.

He was in the chair when I came in.

That is the image I cannot put down. Not the broken doors, not the dust still hanging gold in the lamplight, not the few quiet armed men along the walls who would not meet my eye. Grundrak, in the chair. *My* chair, if you like, though I have stopped saying that even to myself. He had not waited to be invited and he had not waited to be crowned. He had simply sat, as a man sits in a chair, because his legs were tired and there was a chair.

And he said — I have it exactly — *"You didn't come to the demonstration, so I decided to bring the demonstration to you."*

Here is the horror, and it is not the horror you expect. The horror is not that I was beaten. Men are beaten. The horror is that I *understood* him, completely, and agreed. He was right. I had been invited to the demonstration. I had declined, because to attend would have been to concede that the question was open, and I had judged — wrongly, but not foolishly — that a king who attends a demonstration of his own obsolescence has already abdicated. So he brought it to me. The path his charges took, outer wall to inner gate to the doors of my own keep, in a straight devastating line to the chair — *that line was the argument.* There is nothing between us that I cannot go through. He did not need to say it. The rubble said it. It is still saying it.

They let me live, of course. That was the cleverest cruelty, though I do not think he intended it as one; I think he simply had no use for my death and a great use for my continued, ceremonial, smiling existence. I am still King. I open things. I am present at the signing of instruments I do not author. My face is on the coin, and on the coin I am younger, and the young king on the coin is the only one of us still permitted to look as though he decides anything.

The one thing they cannot take is that I notice. He sat in the chair. A man who genuinely did not care about the old hierarchy — and he tells everyone, constantly, that he does not care, that he wishes only for the chair to mean *nothing* — that man would not have needed to sit in it. He needed to. I saw him need to. It is the single fact I possess that he does not, and I keep it, the way a beggar keeps a coin too worn to spend.

—

A decade now. The throne room is busier and quieter both. The mages come, who once would not have been admitted, and stand where my generals stood, and report: this neighbour's soil, that one's gems, the one beyond the river that has not yet learned what we learned, that still kills its dragons and burns wood like our grandfathers. They recommend which shall receive a *demonstration*. I add my voice. My voice is decorative and I add it anyway; it is what I am for.

And there is to be a marriage. My child and his. I am told the children are already easy with one another — that mine, in fact, can hardly be kept from going *below*, rides their tunnels for the pleasure of it, bows to enter without seeming to feel the bow. I watched my own blood stoop through a doorway built too low for us and come up smiling on the other side, and not rub the back of the neck, and not notice, and I understood that the conquest I survived will be completed in people who will never know there was anything to survive.

I notice that too. It is all I do now. I notice, and I dress, and I keep the worn coin in my hand.
