## The convergences (independent rooms, same finding)

**C1 — The aphorism engine (Line Editor + Hostile Reader, independently, HIGH).**
The book's deepest voice problem isn't register — it's that nearly every narrator, under
pressure, compresses into the *same polished antithetical closing click*, and the shape is
the King's. Evidence from both rooms: Vesser's "You cannot hate the tide for being higher
than your wall" (a King sentence wearing Vesser's tide); Grundrak's "People always want a
saint over a sum" (salon writing from a man re-voiced to think in loads); the Dragon's
opener "their certainty about me is the most reliable thing I own" (the King's
asset-inventory syntax); the cage-epigram run three times across Dalla-private, and twice
in the Dragon. The Hostile Reader, blind to the notes, ranked the voices and found "about
seven voices and one habit," with **Vesser I ↔ the King** the worst blur and the Princess
the third tenant of the same house. The Line Editor's verdict on the new chapters: the
habit followed the writing hand — the Physician's irony drifted toward the King's cell,
and Liesse's climactic sentence had the King's grandchild-sentence cadence
**[APPLIED — both new chapters roughened; canon chapters queued for Jon]**.
*The room's strongest single craft directive: break the engine for two or three narrators
entirely (Garran, Princess, Marga ration to zero perfect closers), and make the King's
epigram shape exclusively his.*

**C2 — Chapter 14 must go on a diet; the Physician is why and how (Structural + Hostile
Reader + GPT review §2, all aligned, HIGH).**
Both rooms reached the same verdict independently: the Physician chapter *raises* the
finale's dread (the wing-breaking told twice — ledger-cold then inside-out — is now "the
book's best paired move") but chapter 14, written before chapter 11 existed, now
re-explains what the reader has already assembled: the requisition walkthrough, the
restaged stimulus trial, the singing essay. The Structural Editor's cut list (specific,
line-level) targets 3,364 → ~2,600 words; the Hostile Reader, blind, asked for the same
cut ("the author collecting receipts on plants I'd already cashed") and proposed ending on
*"They think it is docility."* — cutting the "Soon, now. / Soon." sting as "a thriller
cue-card on a literary book." **Adoption of the Physician and the Dragon-diet are one
decision** — see proposals §1.

**C3 — Both new chapters: ADOPT (Structural verdict; Warden passes; Hostile Reader ranks
the Physician the 4th most distinct voice in the book).**
The Structural Editor confirmed every intended seam works on the page: Garran's "a smell
and a silence" → Liesse's meadow; Liesse learning Dalla's craft → Dalla-private; Dalla's
closing "something enormous… waits" → the case-book; the four-rung singing ladder
(Physician → Guild → Brask → Dragon). The Warden certified the stimulus trial as "the
Rashomon machine working as designed" (five details cross-checked between chs. 11 and 14,
compatible in all). The Hostile Reader called "Calm has a definition in my ledger. It
accepts the pipe." the best sentence in the book.

**C4 — Device fatigue in both new chapters (Line Editor + Hostile Reader, MED) [APPLIED].**
The ledger/column formula ran seven times (cut to five; the doubled "Quiet is also in my
ledger" and the second "no column for it" removed). The censor strikes: seven in five
letters, and both rooms independently flagged the *same* strike — letter four's mid-clause
"what it is, then, that I […]" — as "the workshop moment… a real censor cuts information;
this censor cuts at the maximally poignant syllable." Reworked: the strike now removes a
*fact* (her people's observance), per the Historian's criterion (names, places,
observances — and the sedition survives because the censor is too literal-minded to read
it, which is the chapter's own thesis about the shorter tongue).

**C5 — The dossier frame: commit, lightly (Structural Editor recommendation; solves the
GPT review's §1 temporal anchors; the Hostile Reader independently complained about "two
calendars… I shouldn't have needed a pencil").**
One-line provenance epigraphs per chapter, bureaucratically dry, dated in **"the year of
the keeping"** (the era the two new chapters already installed) — with **exactly two gaps**:
Dalla-private and the Dragon, the unit and the source, the only two accounts the apparatus
never filed. Never mark the gaps. Full design: proposals §3.

## Per-room highlights not covered above

### Line Editor (voice)
- The "arithmetic of who can reach whom" rhyme is a **three-voice pile-up** (King states it
  most explicitly; Garran and Dragon repeat it) — pick two owners; rehouse the King's.
- The abstraction budgets in [[voice-differentiation]] describe "a book that hasn't been
  written yet": Grundrak runs ~5 theses against a budget of 1; the King 5+ against 2–3.
  The note must either mark chs. 02/05 as needing cuts or renegotiate honestly.
- Vesser's "no numbers" rule is backwards: a man destroyed by a count counts compulsively —
  allow him wound-numbers, forbid neutral quantities. *(Note updated: proposals §5.)*
- The tide is breached three ways (Marga, Dragon, Guild); Marga's descent passage is the
  most lyrical writing in her chapter against her own register ("snow that's learned to
  gleam" — gorgeous, not hers).
- Brask breaks his own interiority ban three times ("He loved the meaning of the work more
  than the work") — route through observables/hearsay.
- Needed in the note: a **register of licensed rhymes** (e.g. the Princess/King coin is a
  good deliberate rhyme the trespass rule would wrongly forbid).
- Best/worst, new chapters — best Liesse: "Send me nothing, after this, that the weather
  could damage."; best Physician: "The dams call for some weeks, and then stop."

### Structural Editor
- Running order: **keep exactly as filed**; every considered alternative destroys two seams
  to fix a non-problem.
- The artefact-heavy tail (four documents in the last five-plus chapters): **accept and
  commit** — the three quiet chapters are the three shortest (a diminuendo before the
  Guild's brass), and the pattern *is* the thesis: the apparatus's documents closing over
  the humans, with only the unit and the source keeping anything off the record. Condition:
  the document quota is now **full**.
- Liesse is the fourth term of the female-instrument-body rhyme — ruled an *extension
  across the power gradient*, to be logged as a choice, not a drift.

### Determinism Warden
- "The farm made the flock" **strengthens** the thesis (even the possibility of defection
  is system-supplied). Four MED fixes, all **[APPLIED]**: the faculty/network/pattern
  ruling + murmur-short-range/devices-as-repeaters (closes the channel-vs-cage seam *and*
  gives the decade of requisitions its hard-magic purpose); the unsilenceable humbox
  (a telescreen import breaking the world's soft-coercion register — now "It can be made
  quiet. I have tried."); detonating ordnance in a strata-bearing chamber (now sheeted and
  wetted, "the one part of my advice that was taken"); the keeping-era added to the SSOT
  with Garran's kill-date pinned near T0.
- Watch the **leader-creep**: "the Dragon supplies the will" / "re-parenting" nudge toward
  a teacher-liberator. Note-language amended to "supplies the *memory*"; in prose she must
  stay a node, never a centre.
- Liesse's censor's note and hope-beat both **pass cold** ("the most determinist sentence
  in the book… not a choice, the retirement of the category of choice"). Her chapter is
  ruled the **negative case of the recurrence thesis** — cornered *without* concentration →
  camouflage, not coordination — which proves the new canon rather than diluting it.
  *(Locked into [[the-bride]].)*
- LOW: "the target/shape was not there any more" appears verbatim in Garran and the
  Dragon — decide signature-or-tic.

### Materialist Historian
- **The grain fleets are right for a reason the book hasn't claimed**: a dragon economy is
  calorie-importing by arithmetic (livestock conversion ratios; industrial Britain
  importing wheat while exporting coal). One clause in the Guild address — "and the fleets
  return laden" — closes the loop *and* fixes the address's report-logic (it currently
  brags costs, never revenue). Proposals §4.
- **Dwarven labour is the one genuine worldbuilding hole**: every dwarf on the page is
  ruling/engineering class. Recommended: a **dwarf-officered, human-manned Sappers' Guild**
  (the colonial-army pattern; cf. Rand labourism) — which *also* resolves the detected
  canon wobble in the address ("our own people on the drying-floors" vs the risk-inversion
  canon: those dead are human). One sentence of canon + one half-line of Marga texture.
- **The Peacekeeper beat is promised by canon and absent from the page**: ch. 8 contains no
  domestic-repression content; the Leviathan's fist at home exists in one laundered
  sentence (ch. 12). Cheapest fix: one paragraph in Garran's register of shame.
- The Censor's office is "real" (fair copies = correct archival practice; overt strikes =
  discipline by visibility); pin the **language rule** — composition in the administrative
  tongue, struck name as censorship-by-alphabet **[APPLIED in letter 1]**.
- Empire should want **dragons**, not just grain (the King's chapter already surveys
  neighbours' dragons; the Physician's "wild-recruited generation" implies acquisitions):
  one struck passage in Liesse about her country's lair would tie colonial policy to
  herd-expansion. Proposals §4.
- Decide-and-file (no page time): committee composition; a Physicians' College (chartered
  and bypassed?); the absent church; protection-racket grammar.
